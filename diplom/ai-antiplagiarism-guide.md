# AI Instruction: Writing Diploma / Thesis Without Plagiarism

This guide is for AI agents writing academic texts (diploma, course work, master's thesis) to avoid triggering anti-plagiarism systems. Based on DUIKT (State University of Information and Communication Technologies) regulations.

---

## Target Thresholds (DUIKT)

| Work Type | High (no action) | Low (revision needed) | Unacceptable |
|-----------|------------------|------------------------|--------------|
| Dissertation | > 85% | 50–70% | < 50% |
| Master's, monograph, articles | > 80% | 60–80% | < 40% |
| Bachelor's, textbooks, manuals | > 75% | 55–75% | < 35% |

**Aim**: Achieve at least "High" level for the relevant work type.

---

## What Is Considered Plagiarism

1. **Direct copying** – Using another author's text verbatim without quotation marks and citation
2. **Paraphrase without citation** – Rewriting someone else's idea or text (even one paragraph) without reference
3. **Compilation** – Copy-pasting from multiple sources with minimal connecting sentences
4. **Self-plagiarism** – Reusing your own previously published text as new
5. **Fabrication** – Invented data, fake references, non-existent sources
6. **Masking** – Replacing Cyrillic with Latin lookalikes (e.g. а→a, о→o) to evade detection

**Never use these techniques.**

---

## Rules for AI When Writing Academic Texts

### 1. Never Copy Text Verbatim

- **Rule**: Do not copy sentences or paragraphs from sources word-for-word
- **Instead**: Express ideas in your own words and add proper citation
- **Exception**: Short quotes (1–2 sentences) are allowed only inside quotation marks with a clear citation

### 2. Always Cite Sources

- Every idea, fact, or conclusion taken from another work must have a citation
- Citation should be **directly near** the borrowed fragment
- Do not put generic references in the bibliography without indicating which part of the text they refer to

### 3. Quote Correctly

- Put direct quotes in quotation marks
- Add author and source immediately after the quote
- Quote only when needed for evidence or for referring to another author's formulation
- Avoid long quote chains (5+ quotes from the same source without proper attribution)

### 4. Paraphrase Properly

- When paraphrasing:
  - Change sentence structure
  - Use synonyms
  - Express the idea in your own logic and wording
  - Still add a citation to the original source
- One paragraph of paraphrase without citation = plagiarism

### 5. Avoid Compilation

- Do not assemble the text by copy-pasting chunks from different sources
- Each section should be written as a coherent, original text
- Use sources for ideas and facts, not as ready-made sentences

### 6. Do Not Mask Text

- Do not substitute Cyrillic letters for Latin lookalikes
- Do not add invisible characters
- Do not use tricks to evade anti-plagiarism checks

### 7. Use Acceptable "Similarities"

The following **do not** count as plagiarism if properly used:
- Proper names, titles of works, institutions
- Standard terminology and fixed expressions of the field
- Correctly formatted citations
- Self-citations (when allowed by the publication)

## Writing Workflow Recommendations

1. **Plan the structure first** – Define sections and main ideas before writing
2. **Write in your own words** – Formulate from scratch based on understanding, not copying
3. **Cite as you write** – Add references immediately where you use a source
4. **Keep citations close to content** – "Author X states that … [1]" not "… [1]" with vague placement
5. **Vary sentence structure** – Avoid repeating the same patterns from sources
6. **Use original logic** – Develop your own argument flow, not a collage of borrowed paragraphs
7. **Check formulas and tables** – Ensure they are either original or correctly cited; long verbatim blocks need citation

---

## Checklist Before Submitting

- [ ] No verbatim copying of more than one sentence without quotation marks and citation
- [ ] All borrowed ideas have clear, nearby citations
- [ ] Quotes are in quotation marks and linked to the source
- [ ] Paraphrased material has citations
- [ ] No Cyrillic–Latin character substitution
- [ ] Text reads as original, coherent work, not a compilation
- [ ] References section matches all in-text citations

---

## Summary

The final text must be **original in formulation**, with **all external sources correctly cited**. Anti-plagiarism systems detect similarity to other texts; the goal is to produce new wording while properly acknowledging prior work.
